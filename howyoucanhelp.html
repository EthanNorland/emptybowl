Direct Action

From Empathy to Structural Impact

Meaningful support goes beyond canned food drives. It demands knowing what pantries actually need, creating shame-free campus mutual aid, and pushing for policy reforms that secure food as a fundamental human right.

01 · CASH MULTIPLIER

Give Financial Donations

Food banks buy at deep commercial wholesale volume: $1 in cash can purchase up to 3 to 4 times more nutrient-dense food (fresh produce, milk, lean meat) than a consumer buying canned goods at retail price.

02 · CAMPUS & LOCAL

Stock Mutual Aid Fridges

Community fridges and campus lockers offer barrier-free, 24/7 food access without intrusive intake paperwork, photo IDs, or means-testing questions. They operate on solidarity rather than surveillance.

03 · DIGNITY PRINCIPLES

Choice-Based Volunteering

Support pantries designed like grocery stores where visitors choose items that suit their culinary culture, dietary restrictions, and family size, rather than receiving a standardized box of unwanted goods.

04 · POLICY & REFORM

Advocate for Policy Change

Emergency food is a temporary bandage. Real progress requires expanding SNAP eligibility for community college students, funding universal school lunches, and incentivizing grocery stores in food deserts.

The Smart Donor's Field Guide

High-Utility Items to Give

  • Canned proteins (tuna, chicken, salmon, black beans, chickpeas)
  • Nut butters and seed spreads
  • Cooking oils (olive oil, vegetable oil) and whole spices
  • Whole grain pasta, brown rice, rolled oats, and quinoa
  • Menstrual hygiene products, baby diapers, and bar soaps

Items to Avoid Donating

  • Cans with deep dents, rust, or bulging lids (botulism risk)
  • Expired or opened packaging from your home kitchen cabinet
  • Glass jars that can easily shatter in transit and warehouse bins
  • High-sugar junk food, discarded party leftovers, or frosted cakes
  • Items requiring complicated kitchen appliances (many recipients have only a microwave)
