Systemic Anatomy

The Interlocking Drivers of Hunger

Hunger in wealthy nations is not a production shortfall; it is an economic and distribution failure. Food insecurity emerges where rising cost-of-living, stagnant wages, and spatial inequities collide.

The 4 Structural Pillars

01 · ECONOMIC

The Living Wage Gap

Minimum and low wages have remained largely decoupled from the actual cost of living. Grocery prices for essential proteins, fruits, and dairy have risen significantly, while entry-level wages remain stagnant. Even multiple jobs fail to build savings.

02 · GEOGRAPHIC

Food Deserts & Redlining

Major supermarket chains systematically avoid low-income zip codes, a practice known as grocery redlining. In their place, dollar stores and gas-station convenience marts dominate, stocking high-sodium, ultra-processed calories with zero fresh vegetables or meat.

03 · SPATIAL & TRANSIT

Transportation Inequity

A grocery store located three miles away is accessible in seven minutes with a car, but requires multiple bus transfers and hours of travel on foot while carrying perishable goods in extreme weather. Without reliable transit, residents pay premium prices at neighborhood corner stores.

04 · POLICY & CLIFFS

The Benefits Cliff

Safety-net programs feature sharp income cutoffs. An hourly raise of just 50 cents can disqualify a single mother from SNAP benefits, causing an immediate net loss of hundreds of dollars in food support each month. The system penalizes career progression.
